What Is a Machine?

A machine is a device that helps us perform a task more conveniently.

A machine does not magically remove the need for energy. Instead, it can change the amount of force required, the direction of force, or the distance through which the force acts.

💡 WHY?

Imagine lifting a very heavy object directly upward. You may need a large force. A machine can allow you to use a smaller force by making you move through a greater distance.

So the important idea is: machines trade force and distance.

03

Load, Effort & Fulcrum

💪

Effort

The force applied by us to operate the machine.

Why? We supply energy to the machine through the effort.
📦

Load

The resistance or useful object that the machine has to move.

Why? The machine is useful only if it can act on the load.
🔺

Fulcrum

The fixed point around which a lever rotates.

Why? The fulcrum provides the turning point for rotation.
04

Mechanical Advantage

MA = Load Effort

Mechanical Advantage tells us how many times the machine increases the force compared with the effort we apply.

💡 WHY DO WE NEED MA?

Suppose a load requires 300 N of force but you can provide only 100 N. A machine that gives MA = 3 can ideally produce the required force using that smaller effort.

But this does not mean the machine creates energy. Something else must change — usually the distance through which the effort moves.

06

Velocity Ratio

VR = Distance moved by effort Distance moved by load
💡 WHY DISTANCE?

If a machine allows us to use less force, the effort usually has to move through a larger distance.

This is the trade-off that prevents a machine from giving us unlimited force for free.

07

Efficiency

η = MA VR × 100%

Efficiency tells us how effectively the input work is converted into useful output work.

75%
💡 WHY IS EFFICIENCY NOT 100% IN REAL MACHINES?

Real machines have friction, deformation, air resistance and other energy losses.

Some input energy becomes thermal energy, sound or other forms instead of useful output.

Therefore practical machines normally have efficiency below 100%.

08

Lever — The First Great Machine

Why does a lever work?

A lever rotates around a fulcrum. The turning effect depends not only on force but also on the distance from the fulcrum.

Effort × Effort Arm = Load × Load Arm

This is the idea of moment of force. A smaller force can balance a larger force if it acts farther from the fulcrum.

Effort Moment 300 Nm Load Moment 300 Nm ⚖️ Balanced

Both turning effects are equal, so the lever is balanced.

09

Three Classes of Lever

CLASS 1
LOAD 🔺 EFFORT

Fulcrum in the middle

Example: seesaw, scissors, crowbar.

CLASS 2
FULCRUM 📦 EFFORT

Load in the middle

Example: wheelbarrow, nutcracker.

CLASS 3
FULCRUM 💪 LOAD

Effort in the middle

Example: tweezers, human forearm.

Important: A class-3 lever can have MA less than 1. It sacrifices force advantage for greater movement or speed.

Inclined Plane

Why does a ramp make lifting easier?

Instead of lifting an object vertically, we can move it along a longer sloping path.

The required force can decrease because the same change in height occurs over a greater distance.

Ideal MA = Length / Height
💡 The trade-off

Less force is needed, but the object travels a greater distance.

💡 WHY CAN'T A MACHINE CREATE ENERGY?

Energy cannot simply appear from nowhere. A machine can redistribute the input energy between force, distance, speed and direction.

In real machines, some energy is also transformed into heat, sound and other forms because of friction and deformation.

15

Common Misconceptions

❌

"A machine creates energy."

Correct: A machine transfers and transforms energy.

❌

"Every machine multiplies force."

Correct: Some machines trade force advantage for speed, distance or movement.

❌

"MA is always greater than 1."

Correct: MA can be less than 1, equal to 1 or greater than 1.

❌

"Real machines are 100% efficient."

Correct: Real machines lose some energy through friction, deformation and other effects.

Chapter Summary

  • A machine makes a task more convenient by changing force, distance or direction.
  • Effort is the force supplied to a machine.
  • Load is the resistance or useful object being moved.
  • MA = Load / Effort
  • VR = Distance moved by effort / Distance moved by load
  • Efficiency = MA / VR × 100%
  • Levers rotate around a fulcrum.
  • Machines do not create energy.
  • Real machines lose some input energy through friction and other effects.
⚙️ The Big Idea

A machine does not give us something for nothing. It gives us a useful trade-off between force, distance, speed and direction.
