THE BASIC IDEA

What Is Force?

🔵

What?

A force is a push or pull that can change the motion or shape of an object.

💡

Why do we need force?

An object does not automatically change its motion. To accelerate, slow down, stop, start or change direction, an unbalanced force is required.

⚙️

How does it work?

A force interacts with an object's mass. The result of this interaction is a change in velocity, which we call acceleration.

🧠 Think About It

Imagine a football lying on the ground.

Ball at rest → Kick it → Force acts → Velocity changes

WHY? Because the kick provides an external force that changes the ball's motion.

FORCES AROUND YOU

Different Forces

Forces may look different, but they can all be represented as pushes or pulls.

🫸

Applied Force

A force directly applied by a person or object.

Example: pushing a box.
🌍

Gravity

The attractive force between masses.

Example: Earth pulling objects downward.
🧤

Friction

A force that opposes relative motion between surfaces.

Example: brakes slowing a bicycle.
🪢

Tension

A pulling force transmitted through a stretched string, rope or cable.

Example: a hanging lamp supported by a cable.
⬆️

Normal Force

A support force exerted by a surface on an object.

Example: a table supporting a book.
🧲

Magnetic Force

A non-contact force produced by magnetic fields.

Example: a magnet attracting iron.
NEWTON'S FIRST LAW

Why doesn't an object simply change its motion?

Newton's First Law describes inertia.

An object resists a change in its state of motion.

But why?

If the net external force is zero, there is no unbalanced interaction capable of changing the object's velocity.

Newton's First Law condition ΣF = 0 No net force → no acceleration
REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE
🚗

A moving car tends to keep moving unless an external unbalanced force changes its motion.

THE MATHEMATICAL CONNECTION

Newton's Second Law

❓ Why does F = ma exist?

Newton's second law tells us how the motion of an object responds to an unbalanced force.

Force depends on change in momentum F ∝ Δp / Δt
↓
Momentum p = mv
↓
For constant mass F = m × Δv / Δt
↓
Acceleration F = ma

WHY? The larger the force, the greater the change in velocity per second. The larger the mass, the harder it is to produce the same acceleration.

EQUILIBRIUM

Static vs Dynamic

🧱

Static Equilibrium

The object is at rest and the net force is zero.

Book resting on a table.
v = 0 ΣF = 0
🚆

Dynamic Equilibrium

The object moves with constant velocity while the net force remains zero.

Train moving at constant velocity on a straight track.
v = constant ΣF = 0
⚠️

Important: Zero net force does NOT necessarily mean the object is stationary. It means its acceleration is zero.

THINK LIKE A PHYSICIST

Why Questions

Why does a heavier object need more force?

For the same acceleration, F = ma. Increasing mass increases the required force.

Why does an object stop when friction acts?

Friction acts opposite to motion. If it produces a net force opposite to the velocity, the object slows down.

Why can many forces act without acceleration?

Because forces are vectors. Opposite forces can cancel so that the net force becomes zero.

Why is normal force upward?

A surface prevents an object from passing through it, producing a contact force perpendicular to the surface.

Why is force a vector?

A force has both magnitude and direction. Two equal forces in different directions can therefore have completely different effects.

Why doesn't zero force mean zero velocity?

Force controls acceleration, not velocity directly. If net force is zero, velocity remains constant.

DON'T MEMORISE THE WRONG IDEA

Common Misconceptions

✕

"If an object is moving, there must be a force in the direction of motion."

✓ Not necessarily. Constant velocity can occur when net force is zero.
✕

"If forces are acting, the object must accelerate."

✓ Only an unbalanced net force produces acceleration.
✕

"Action and reaction cancel each other."

✓ They act on different objects, so they do not cancel on the same free body diagram.
