01 · CONCEPT

What is Circular Motion?

🌀 Simple idea

When an object moves along a circular path around a fixed centre, its motion is called circular motion.

Examples
  • Fan blades rotating
  • A stone tied to a string
  • A car turning around a bend
  • A satellite orbiting Earth
  • Washing machine drum
WHY?

Why can't the object simply continue straight?

An object naturally tends to continue in the direction in which it is already moving.

To make its path curve, something must continuously change the direction of its velocity.

That continuous change of direction is the key to understanding circular motion.

The radius becomes extremely important because both acceleration and centripetal force depend on it.

03 · ANGULAR MOTION

Angular Displacement & Angular Speed

01

Angular displacement

θ = Δs / r

Instead of describing how far the object travelled along the path, we can describe how much it rotated around the centre.

02

Angular speed

ω = Δθ / Δt

Angular speed tells us how quickly the object is rotating.

WHY? More angle covered in less time means faster rotation.

05 · SPEED

Tangential Speed

IMPORTANT RELATION
v = rω

Why does radius affect speed?

Imagine two objects rotating with exactly the same angular speed.

The object farther from the centre travels a larger distance during the same rotation.

Therefore, for the same angular speed, a larger radius produces a larger tangential speed.

06 · THE BIG IDEA

Constant Speed ≠ Constant Velocity

WHY?

The object can have constant speed, but its velocity still changes.

Speed tells us only how fast the object is moving.

Velocity tells us both:

  • how fast it moves
  • which direction it moves

During circular motion, the direction of motion changes continuously.

Direction changes → Velocity changes → Acceleration exists
07 · ACCELERATION

Centripetal Acceleration

MAIN FORMULA
ac = v² / r
ac = rω²

What does it mean?

Centripetal acceleration is the acceleration directed continuously toward the centre of the circular path.

Centre ← acceleration
WHY?

Why toward the centre?

If the velocity needs to continuously turn toward the new direction of the circular path, the change in velocity points inward.

Therefore the acceleration responsible for this change points toward the centre.

08 · FORCE

Centripetal Force

Fc = mv² / r

Here's the logic

Circular path ↓ Velocity direction changes ↓ Acceleration points inward ↓ Net force must point inward
⚠️ Important: Centripetal force is not a new separate type of force.

It is simply the net inward force required for circular motion.

🪢 Tension 🛞 Friction 🌍 Gravity ⬆️ Normal force

10 · EXPERIMENT

What Happens When Speed Increases?

Mass = 2 kg
Radius = 3 m
Speed 5 m/s
v² 25
Force 16.67 N
WHY?

Because centripetal force depends on v², increasing speed increases the required force much faster than speed itself.

11 · EXPERIMENT

What Happens When Radius Changes?

Mass = 2 kg
Speed = 6 m/s
Radius 2 m
Acceleration 18 m/s²
Force 36 N
WHY?

At constant speed, increasing the radius gives the object more distance to turn, so the required inward acceleration decreases.

12 · REAL WORLD

Circular Motion Around Us

🚗

Car Turning

Friction between tyres and road can provide the inward force required to turn the car.

WHY? Without enough inward force, the car tends to continue along its previous direction.
🪢

Stone + String

Tension in the string pulls the stone toward the centre.

WHY? Remove the string's inward pull and the stone no longer has the required centripetal force.
🌀

Washing Machine

The drum rotates and continuously changes the direction of objects inside it.

WHY? The rotating drum provides the forces that change the motion of the clothes.
🌍

Satellites

Gravity can provide the inward force needed for orbital motion.

WHY? Gravity continuously changes the direction of the satellite's velocity.

14 · MISCONCEPTIONS

Things You Should NOT Memorize Wrong

✕

"Centripetal force is a new type of force."

No. It is the name we give to the net inward force required for circular motion.

✕

"Constant speed means no acceleration."

Not in circular motion. Velocity changes because direction changes.

✕

"Larger radius always means larger force."

It depends on what remains constant. For fixed speed, increasing radius reduces the required force.

✕

"The outward force keeps the object in the circle."

In an ordinary inertial-frame analysis, the required centripetal net force is inward.
