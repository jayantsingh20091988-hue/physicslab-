START WITH REAL LIFE

Why does a stone fall when you release it?

🪨

Observation

Release a stone and it accelerates toward Earth.

❓

The question

Something must be producing a force on the stone. But where is that force coming from?

🌍

The idea

Earth attracts the stone. This attraction is called gravitational force.

💡 WHY does it accelerate?

Because a force causes a change in motion. Earth's gravitational pull acts on the stone, so its velocity changes toward Earth.

This is the same basic connection we learned earlier:

Force → Acceleration → Change in motion
CONCEPT

What is Gravitation?

🌌

Simple meaning

Gravitation is the attractive force that exists between any two objects having mass.

🌍

On Earth

Earth attracts objects toward its centre. That is why dropped objects move downward.

🌙

In space

The same gravitational interaction helps keep the Moon around Earth and planets around the Sun.

The important insight:

Gravity is not a special force that exists only near the ground. It acts between masses throughout space.

NEWTON'S LAW

Universal Law of Gravitation

Newton described how the gravitational force depends on the masses of two objects and the distance between them.

F = G m₁m₂ r²

This means gravitational force increases when masses increase, but decreases rapidly when distance increases.

Mass ↑

More mass means stronger gravitational attraction.

F ∝ m₁m₂

Distance ↑

Increasing separation weakens gravitational force.

F ∝ 1/r²

Distance × 2

If distance doubles, force becomes one-fourth.

F → F/4

💡 WHY is distance squared?

In the simplest physical picture, the gravitational influence spreads outward through space. As distance increases, the same influence is distributed over a larger spherical surface.

The surface area of a sphere grows as:

A = 4πr²

That geometric spreading is connected to the inverse-square relationship seen in gravity.

EARTH'S GRAVITY

Why do different objects fall with similar acceleration?

Gravitational force near Earth

F = mg

Here g represents acceleration due to gravity.

Newton's second law

F = ma

If gravity is the main force acting on an object, we can compare these equations.

Let's see where g comes from

Gravitational force F = GMm/r²
Newton's second law F = ma
Equate them ma = GMm/r²
Cancel m a = GM/r²
Near Earth's surface g = GM/R²

Notice something important: the object's own mass m cancels out. That is why, when air resistance is negligible, different objects have the same gravitational acceleration at the same location.

FREE FALL

Free Fall — what actually happens?

An object is in free fall when gravity is the main force acting on it.

💡 WHY does the object get faster?

Gravity continuously accelerates the object downward. During every small interval of time, its velocity changes. Therefore the object covers more distance in each successive second.

v = u + gt s = ut + ½gt²
IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Mass ≠ Weight

These two words are often used as if they mean the same thing. They do not.

⚖️

Mass

Mass measures the amount of matter in an object and is related to its inertia.

Unit: kg

Your mass remains approximately the same on Earth and Moon.

🌍

Weight

Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.

W = mg

Your weight changes when the local gravitational acceleration changes.

Weight on different worlds

Earth 490.5 N

Your mass stays the same, but Earth's gravitational acceleration determines your weight.

EXPLORE DEEPER

What happens to gravity as we go higher?

Gravity becomes weaker as distance from Earth's centre increases.

Distance from Earth's centre 6371 km
Approx. g 9.81 m/s²
Relative gravity 100%

💡 WHY does g decrease?

Because you are farther from Earth's centre. Since gravitational acceleration follows an inverse-square relationship with distance:

g = GM/r²

Increasing r makes g smaller.

Gravity does not simply keep increasing as you move toward Earth's centre.

CONNECT THE CHAPTERS

So why doesn't the Moon fall onto Earth?

01

The Moon has forward motion

The Moon is not stationary. It has tangential velocity.

02

Earth pulls inward

Earth's gravity continuously changes the Moon's direction.

03

The path bends

Instead of moving in a straight line, the Moon follows a curved orbital path.

Gravity bends the Moon's trajectory toward Earth.
Important:

Saying “the Moon is falling” is only useful if we explain what it means: gravity is continuously bending its path inward while its forward motion prevents it from simply dropping onto Earth.

REAL WORLD

Where do we use gravitation?

🛰️

Satellites

Earth's gravity provides the inward acceleration required for orbital motion.

🌊

Tides

Gravitational interactions involving the Moon and Sun contribute to ocean tides.

🌎

Atmosphere

Earth's gravity helps keep gases bound around the planet.

🚀

Space travel

Rockets must change their motion to move between different gravitational environments and orbits.

THINK CAREFULLY

Common misconceptions

✕

“Gravity only exists on Earth.”

Gravity acts between masses everywhere.

✕

“Heavy objects always fall faster.”

Ignoring air resistance, objects near Earth's surface have approximately the same gravitational acceleration.

✕

“Mass and weight are the same.”

Mass is measured in kg; weight is a gravitational force measured in newtons.

✕

“Gravity becomes stronger when you go higher.”

Increasing distance from Earth's centre decreases gravitational acceleration.

✕

“The Moon has no gravity.”

The Moon has gravity. It is weaker than Earth's because the Moon has much less mass.
