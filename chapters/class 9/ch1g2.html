KINEMATICS • MODULE 01

Motion & Reference Frame

Motion is not simply about an object moving. Before describing motion, we must first decide relative to what the motion is being observed.

01

What Is Motion?

An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with time relative to its surroundings or to a chosen reference point.

🤔 Think First

Imagine you are sitting inside a moving train. You look at your friend sitting beside you.

Is your friend moving?

Now look outside the window at a tree. Is the tree moving?

Your answer depends on the reference frame.

02

Rest and Motion Are Relative

There is no universal meaning of "at rest" or "moving" without specifying the reference frame.

At Rest

Position does not change with time relative to the chosen reference frame.

🪑 A passenger sitting inside a train is at rest relative to the train.

In Motion

Position changes with time relative to the chosen reference frame.

🚆 The same passenger is moving relative to a person standing on the platform.
03

Reference Frame

A reference frame is the system relative to which we describe the position and motion of an object.

Core idea
Motion = Change of position relative to a reference frame

🚆 Interactive Reference Frame

Change the observer's frame and see how the same object can appear to behave differently.

Observation: The passenger moves relative to the ground.
04

Relative Motion

The velocity of an object depends on the observer. Therefore, two observers can measure different velocities for the same object.

Relative velocity
vA/B = vA − vB

🚆 Real World Example

A train moves at 60 km/h and a passenger walks forward inside the train at 5 km/h relative to the train.

Relative to the ground, the passenger's velocity becomes:

60 + 5 = 65 km/h

But if the passenger walks backward at 5 km/h:

60 − 5 = 55 km/h
05

Particle / Point Object

In many physics problems, the size and shape of an object are irrelevant compared with the distance travelled.

We can then treat the object as a particle or point object.

🌍 Earth around Sun

For studying Earth's orbital motion, its size can often be ignored.

🚗 Car on Highway

For calculating the distance travelled over a long road, the car can often be treated as a point.

06

Types of Motion

Motion can be classified according to the path followed and the way different parts of the object move.

➡️ Translational Motion

The body changes its position from one place to another.

Example: A car moving on a road.

📏 Rectilinear Motion

Translational motion along a straight line.

Example: A train moving on a straight track.

〰️ Curvilinear Motion

Motion along a curved path.

Example: A ball hit by a batsman.

🔄 Rotational Motion

A body rotates about an axis.

Example: A ceiling fan.

⭕ Circular Motion

Motion along a circular path.

Example: A stone tied to a string.

↔️ Oscillatory Motion

Repeated to-and-fro motion about a mean position.

Example: Pendulum of a clock.

🌫️ Random Motion

Motion without a definite predictable path.

Example: Dust particles in air.

09

Physics Thinking: What Is Actually Moving?

Scenario

You are sitting inside a bus moving at constant speed. Another bus beside you moves in the same direction at almost the same speed.

For a moment, the other bus appears stationary.

Why?

Your position relative to the other bus is changing very slowly.

Therefore, its relative velocity with respect to you is close to zero.

10

Common Conceptual Traps

❌ "Everything has an absolute state of rest."

Not necessarily. Rest depends on the reference frame.

❌ "Circular motion means rotational motion."

Not always. A particle can move in a circle without rotating about its own axis.

❌ "A moving object must have changing speed."

No. Uniform motion can have constant speed.

❌ "If velocity is zero, the object can never move."

An object can have zero velocity at an instant and still begin moving afterward.

Challenge Zone 🧠

These questions are designed to test understanding, not memorisation.

Question 1

A passenger is sitting inside a train moving east. Another passenger walks west inside the train with the same speed as the train.

Relative to the ground, what happens to the second passenger?

Question 2 — Advanced

Two cars move in the same direction with speeds 72 km/h and 54 km/h. A passenger in the faster car observes the slower car. What speed does the slower car appear to have?

Question 3 — IIT-Level Thinking

A particle is moving in a circular path with constant speed. Is the particle accelerating?

12

Module Summary

  • Motion means change in position with time relative to a reference frame.
  • Rest and motion are relative concepts.
  • Reference frame decides how motion is described.
  • Relative velocity compares the velocities of two objects.
  • A sufficiently small or irrelevant-sized object can be treated as a particle.
  • Motion can be translational, rotational, circular, oscillatory or random.
  • The path of motion provides important information about the type of motion.
