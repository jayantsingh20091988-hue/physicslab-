FOUNDATION

Motion & Reference Frame

Motion is not an absolute idea. It depends on what you choose as your reference.

CORE IDEA

What is motion?

An object is said to be in motion when its position changes with time relative to a chosen reference point or reference frame.

LOGIC

No position + time comparison = no meaningful statement about motion.

WHY?

Why is motion relative?

Imagine sitting inside a smoothly moving train. The passenger beside you appears stationary, while a person standing on the platform appears to move backward.

Motion → depends on reference frame

02
GEOMETRY OF MOTION

Distance & Displacement

One tells you how much path was travelled. The other tells you how far the final position is from the initial position, with direction.

DISTANCE
d = total path length

Distance is a scalar quantity. Direction is not required.

DISPLACEMENT
Δx = x₂ − x₁

Displacement is a vector quantity. Direction matters.

WHY DIFFERENT?

Path vs final position

Suppose you walk 100 m east and then 100 m west.

100 m → ← 100 m
DISTANCE 200 m
DISPLACEMENT 0 m
IMPORTANT

Can displacement be greater than distance?

No.

|Displacement| ≤ Distance

Equality occurs only when the object moves along a straight path without reversing direction.

03
RATE OF TRAVEL

Speed & Average Speed

Speed tells us how quickly distance is being covered. But average speed must be calculated from total distance and total time.

CORE FORMULA
Speed = Distance / Time
AVERAGE
vavg speed = Total distance / Total time
WHY NOT JUST AVERAGE THE SPEEDS?

Because time spent at each speed may be different.

Suppose a car travels one part of a journey at 20 km/h and another part at 80 km/h. The average speed is not automatically 50 km/h.

Average speed = Total distance ÷ Total time

REAL-WORLD LOGIC

Why traffic makes average speed tricky

A car may travel at 60 km/h on an empty road and only 20 km/h in traffic. Its journey average depends on how long it actually spends in each situation.

Total distance ÷ Total journey time = Average speed

VECTOR DESCRIPTION

Velocity & Average Velocity

Velocity is not merely “speed with a different name”. It tells us the rate at which position changes.

AVERAGE VELOCITY
vavg = Δx / Δt

It depends on displacement, not total distance.

KEY DIFFERENCE
Speed Scalar
Velocity Vector

DEEP LOGIC

Can speed be non-zero while average velocity is zero?

Yes.

If an object moves and returns to its starting point, the total distance is non-zero but its displacement is zero. Therefore its average velocity is zero.

Return to start → Δx = 0 → vavg = 0

LIMITING IDEA

Instantaneous Velocity

Average velocity tells us what happened over an interval. Instantaneous velocity asks: “What is happening right now?”

What is the velocity at one exact instant?

We cannot simply divide displacement by zero. Instead, we take an extremely small time interval and see what the average velocity approaches.

v = lim Δt → 0 Δx / Δt

WHY DOES THE DERIVATIVE APPEAR?

From average velocity to instantaneous velocity

01

Start with average velocity:

vavg = Δx / Δt
02

Make the time interval smaller.

Δt → smaller
03

The secant line on an x-t graph approaches the tangent line.

Secant → Tangent
04

The limiting slope becomes instantaneous velocity.

v = dx/dt
