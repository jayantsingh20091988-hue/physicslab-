TWO-DIMENSIONAL MOTION

Projectile Motion

What is a projectile?

When an object is projected into the air and after projection the main force acting on it is gravity, its motion is called projectile motion.

A cricket ball hit into the air, a basketball thrown toward a hoop, water coming out of a fountain and a stone thrown from a cliff are everyday examples.

WHY?

Why does the path become curved?

Because the object simultaneously moves horizontally while gravity continuously changes its vertical velocity.

KEY IDEA

Separate the motion

Horizontal and vertical motions can be analysed independently.

REAL WORLD

Sports

The same mathematics helps explain the path of a football, cricket ball or basketball.

Resolve the initial velocity

ux = u cos θ
uy = u sin θ

Why? Because the initial velocity vector can be resolved into two perpendicular components.

Deriving the projectile equations

Horizontal direction

There is no horizontal acceleration when air resistance is neglected.

ax = 0
x = u cosθ × t
Vertical direction

Gravity acts downward.

ay = −g
y = u sinθ × t − ½gt²
Velocity after time t
vx = u cosθ
vy = u sinθ − gt
Time of flight T = 2u sinθ / g
Maximum height H = u²sin²θ / 2g
Horizontal range R = u²sin2θ / g

MOTION ALONG A CURVE

Uniform Circular Motion

Constant speed does NOT mean constant velocity

In uniform circular motion, the magnitude of velocity remains constant, but its direction continuously changes.

Since velocity is a vector quantity, changing its direction means that velocity is changing.

🎡 Ferris wheel

A passenger continuously changes direction while moving around the circle.

🛰️ Satellite

Orbital motion requires continuous change in velocity direction.

🚗 Turning car

A car turning around a bend accelerates even if its speedometer shows the same speed.

Centripetal acceleration

ac = v²/r

The acceleration points toward the centre of the circle.

Why does v²/r appear?

Consider two nearby positions on a circular path. The velocity vectors have equal magnitude but slightly different directions.

The change in velocity depends on the angle through which the velocity vector turns.

For a small angular displacement:

Δv / v ≈ Δs / r

Since:

Δs = vΔt

therefore:

Δv / Δt = v²/r

Hence:

ac = v²/r

OSCILLATORY MOTION

Simple Pendulum

Why does a pendulum return?

When the bob is displaced from its equilibrium position, gravity has a component that acts toward the equilibrium position.

This restoring tendency causes oscillation.

EXTREME POSITION

Velocity = 0

At the extreme position the bob momentarily stops before moving back.

MEAN POSITION

Speed is maximum

Gravitational potential energy has converted into kinetic energy.

ENERGY

KE ↔ PE

Energy continuously transfers between kinetic and potential forms.

Time period

T = 2π√(L/g)

This relation is valid for small angular oscillations.

Why does length matter?

A longer pendulum needs more time to complete one oscillation. The restoring acceleration and the geometry of the circular arc lead to a characteristic time scale proportional to √L.

Gravity provides the restoring influence, so stronger gravity makes the oscillation faster.

T ∝ √L
T ∝ 1/√g

Combining these dependencies:

T ∝ √(L/g)

The full mathematical derivation gives:

T = 2π√(L/g)

DEEPER CONNECTION

From Pendulum to Simple Harmonic Motion

What makes a motion oscillatory?

An object oscillates when it repeatedly moves around an equilibrium position.

In ideal simple harmonic motion, the restoring acceleration is proportional to displacement and directed toward equilibrium.

a ∝ −x
DISPLACEMENT

Farther from centre → stronger restoring tendency

The negative sign indicates that acceleration acts opposite to displacement.

ENERGY

KE ↔ PE

At the centre, kinetic energy is maximum. At maximum displacement, potential energy is maximum.

EXAMPLES

Mass-spring systems

A spring-mass system is a classic approximation of SHM.

Ideal SHM equation

x = A cos(ωt + φ)
v = −Aω sin(ωt + φ)
a = −Aω² cos(ωt + φ)

Since x = A cos(ωt + φ):

a = −ω²x

Why is the acceleration negative?

Suppose the particle is displaced to the right. The restoring force must act toward the centre, therefore towards the left.

If displacement is positive, acceleration becomes negative. If displacement is negative, acceleration becomes positive.

a = −ω²x

The minus sign therefore carries physical information about the direction of the restoring acceleration.
