01 · DIRECTION

Sign Convention

Before solving a motion problem, first decide: which direction is positive?

01

Why do we need signs?

Physics equations do not automatically know which direction you call positive.

You create a coordinate system first. Then every displacement, velocity and acceleration is described relative to that choice.

Example Upward = + Downward = −
02

The important rule

The physical motion does not change when you change your coordinate system.

Only the numerical signs in your description change.

Same physics. Different coordinate description.

Why can negative acceleration still mean speeding up?

Suppose the positive direction is upward.

A ball moving downward has:

v < 0

Gravity also points downward:

a = −g

Both velocity and acceleration are negative. Their directions are the same.

Therefore the ball speeds up while moving downward.

So:

Negative acceleration ≠ automatically slowing down
02 · GRAVITY

Acceleration Due to Gravity

Near Earth's surface, freely falling objects accelerate approximately at

g ≈ 9.8 m/s²

For many school calculations, we use g = 10 m/s².

What does 9.8 m/s² mean?

It does not mean that the object travels 9.8 metres every second.

It means the velocity changes by about 9.8 m/s every second in the downward direction.

0 s 0 m/s
1 s 9.8 m/s
2 s 19.6 m/s
3 s 29.4 m/s

Why does mass not appear in g?

In ideal free fall, gravitational force is

F = GMm / r²

Newton's second law says:

F = ma

Equating them:

ma = GMm / r²

Cancel m:

a = GM / r²

Therefore ideal gravitational acceleration is independent of the object's mass.

Air resistance can change the real-world motion.

Derivation — Maximum Height

At maximum height, the object stops moving upward for one instant.

v = 0

Start with:

v = u − gt

At maximum height:

0 = u − gt

Therefore:

tₐ = u/g

Now use:

v² = u² − 2gH

At maximum height, v = 0:

0 = u² − 2gH

Therefore:

H = u² / 2g
TIME OF ASCENT tₐ = u/g

Time required to reach maximum height.

TIME OF DESCENT tᵈ = u/g

For return to the same level without air resistance.

TOTAL FLIGHT T = 2u/g

Only when the object returns to the original height.

Important observation

At maximum height:

v = 0

But acceleration is still:

a = −g

Gravity did not disappear. The velocity simply becomes zero for an instant before reversing direction.

08–12 · CLASS 11 BRIDGE

Circular Motion

Here something surprising happens: speed can remain constant while acceleration exists.

v → tangent a → centre
Speed 10.00 m/s
Velocity direction Tangential
Centripetal acceleration 1.00 m/s²
WHY?

Constant speed, but acceleration?

Acceleration means change in velocity.

a = Δv / Δt

Velocity is a vector. It contains:

  • Magnitude
  • Direction

During circular motion, speed can remain constant, but the direction of velocity continuously changes.

Therefore velocity changes → acceleration exists.

REAL WORLD

Where do we see this?

  • Car turning on a circular road
  • Fan blades
  • Washing machine drum
  • Stone tied to a string
  • Amusement rides
  • Rotating wheels
  • Satellite-like orbital motion
Derivation — Centripetal Acceleration

Consider an object moving around a circle of radius r with constant speed v.

In a short time Δt, its velocity changes by Δv.

From the geometry of similar triangles:

Δv / v = Δr / r

Rearranging:

Δv = v(Δr/r)

During a short time interval:

Δr = vΔt

Therefore:

Δv = v(vΔt/r)

Divide by Δt:

Δv/Δt = v²/r

Since:

a = Δv/Δt

We obtain:

ac = v²/r

The direction is toward the centre.

CLASS 11 BRIDGE

Angular Speed & v = rω

Angular displacement

If an object moves through an angle θ:

θ = s/r

where s is arc length.

Angular speed

Angular speed is the rate of change of angular displacement.

ω = dθ/dt

Deriving v = rω

s = rθ

Differentiate with respect to time:

ds/dt = r(dθ/dt)

Since:

ds/dt = v

and:

dθ/dt = ω
v = rω
Linear speed v = rω
Centripetal acceleration ac = rω²
Angular speed ω = 2π/T
Frequency f = 1/T
Linear speed v = 2πr/T
Frequency form v = 2πrf
PERIOD

Time for one complete revolution

T = time / number of revolutions
FREQUENCY

Number of revolutions per second

f = 1/T
ANGULAR SPEED

Angle covered per second

ω = 2πf
Class 11 enrichment — Tangential acceleration

If the speed itself is changing during circular motion, there can be another component of acceleration.

at = rα

where α is angular acceleration.

The centripetal component is:

ac = rω²

If both components are perpendicular:

a = √(at² + ac²)

This is an enrichment beyond the basic Class 10 level.

13 · CONCEPT TRAPS

Things That Look Easy — But Aren't

01

Highest point

At maximum height v = 0, but acceleration is still g downward.

02

Negative acceleration

Negative acceleration does not automatically mean the object is slowing down.

03

Circular motion

Constant speed does not imply zero acceleration. Direction can change.

04

Gravity sign

g is not inherently positive or negative. Its sign depends on the chosen coordinate direction.

05

Mass in free fall

Ideal gravitational acceleration does not depend on mass.

06

Frequency vs period

More revolutions per second means higher frequency and smaller period.

07

Speed vs velocity

Speed is scalar. Velocity contains direction.

08

Radians

In SI, radian is dimensionless, but it still represents angular measure.
